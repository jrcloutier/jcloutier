Investigative series

The Illinois Pension Crisis

Chicago Tribune  ·  2024–2025

A multi-part investigation into the structural causes and political failures behind Illinois's $200 billion unfunded pension liability — one of the worst in the nation. The series traced how decades of deferred payments, inflated benefit promises, and legislative dysfunction compounded into a fiscal crisis affecting the livelihoods of hundreds of thousands of public workers and retirees.

Installments

  1. Part 1 March 14, 2024
    How Illinois Spent 30 Years Kicking the Can

    A reconstruction of the legislative decisions that ballooned Illinois's pension debt from manageable to catastrophic.

  2. Part 2 April 2, 2024
    The Beneficiaries: Who Gets What — and Why It's So Hard to Change

    An analysis of pension benefit structures, spiking practices, and why reforms have failed in court after court.

  3. Part 3 May 19, 2024
    The Local Squeeze: How Pension Debt Is Gutting City Services

    From Rockford to Chicago's South Side, municipalities are cutting parks, libraries, and staff to make pension payments.

  4. Part 4 September 8, 2024
    Paths Out: What Other States Have Done and Whether It Could Work Here

    A comparative look at pension reform efforts in New Jersey, Kentucky, and Rhode Island and what lessons Illinois has ignored.

  5. Data analysis Ongoing
    Track Illinois's Pension Debt Over Time

    An interactive database of pension fund balances, employer contribution rates, and long-term projections across all five state systems.
